## What the pilot has revealed

The directory's grouped roster and Jobs destinations were discoverable to the
administrator. The Commander reached agenda preparation through Officer tools and
used a meeting-specific discussion rather than modifying the reusable catalog.
The minutes status explained when members could read the document and who acted
next. The direct Adjutant path and reviewed Commander handoff both completed.

The corrected final copy preserved the actual Meeting title, schedule, body, attendance,
and factual content. Prior revision payload digests and provenance were unchanged.
The UI offered no edit controls after final locking. The direct-origin record remained
attested awaiting approval because no later decision was supplied for it.

Authority followed dated offices: ending Jordan's term removed current agenda, minutes,
and attestation capabilities; Riley's new term supplied them without manual grants.
The complete saved meeting/revision evidence was identical before and after turnover
and a further server restart. Historical Jordan attendance and attestation remained.

Jordan's actual existing signed-in session lost Officer tools and was denied on reload
and direct revisit of the previously observed minutes workspace. The account could
still follow Meetings to the corrected official member copy. No new authentication or
saved-state load was used for this revocation check.

The supervisor separately signed in as Riley through the offline email UI. Officer
tools showed ADJUTANT and the scoped account/settings boundary; the official minutes
workspace remained locked, with no document edits offered to the incoming officer.
This was a conformance check, not another source-blind usability study.

These observations support the current direction; they do not establish usability
for an infrequent officer or a person with low computer confidence. Questions to take
to actual officers are:

- Do officers naturally start in Meetings or Officer tools, and is that distinction
  clear when a draft is not yet available to members?
- Does the short-minutes add-item flow fit the Adjutant's normal practice without an
  agenda or transcript? Is choosing a kind for an adjournment entry understandable?
- Is the confirmation explanation clear on a phone, especially when the email arrives
  on a different device?
- Is roster freshness sufficiently visible where administrators expect it, and does
  the import screen adequately explain sign-in removal?

No reproduced application defect has been established by the completed stages.
Candidates and environment boundaries remain separate below.

## Harness correction and tooling boundaries

The initial pilot server used Rails' UTC default while the fictional Post used
America/Chicago. Two actors observed inconsistent dates across Dashboard and Meetings.
This was a supervisor setup mistake: [deployment guidance](../../DEPLOYMENT.md)
requires the installation and Organization zones to match. The wrapper now sets the
matching zone. Work paused before mutation; actors reloaded after restart; saved
records and authentication survived. Subsequent reads showed the supplied local dates
without editing any schedule. The discrepancy is retained as setup evidence, not a
reported product defect.

Some browser clicks outside the current viewport returned Done without the expected
visible transition. Scrolling the target into view and refreshing snapshots recovered
the workflow. The Adjutant used the email link after code-entry difficulty. These
observations did not establish an application sign-in or agenda-preparation defect.
The existing authentication-entry browser tests independently passed: 2 runs,
33 assertions, zero failures, errors, or skips (`AuthenticationEntrySystemTest`, seed
52053, separate `legion_workspace_ui_test` database).

Starting a recording reset an unsaved approval form and the viewport. Required-meeting
validation stopped that incomplete submission; the actor re-entered the supplied
decision and completed confirmation. Later recording began before form entry, followed
by restoring the intended viewport. This is recorded as a tooling interruption.

Jobs warned that worker health was unavailable. Independent reads confirmed that
the four Solid Queue health tables are absent from the dedicated test database.
The pilot runs no Solid Queue worker and executes only offline mail jobs; the warning
is an expected runtime boundary. Empty job lists do not prove background processing
or live queue health.
